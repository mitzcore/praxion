5. Where people overpay now, and what changes

  1. Tuning is a human service call. A loop is tuned at commissioning by an integrator and re-tuned only after complaints. Loop-tuning audits are sold as consulting engagements. Praxion makes re-tuning an automatic, audited event triggered by the machine's own drift.
  2. Operators switch automation off. About 30% of loops in manual means a person is doing the controller's job by hand, every shift. A controller that notices it is wrong and repairs itself is the alternative to switching it off.
  3. Energy burns silently. Oscillating and sluggish loops waste 5 to 15% of energy in process plants and up to 20% in building HVAC. Nobody is paid to notice.
  4. Appliance vendors ship one tuning for every home. Every heat pump, EV charger and oven is tuned for a nominal unit, then installed into a different duct, wiring and climate, and ages differently. Field adaptation is a support cost the vendor eats today.
  5. Old equipment has no vendor. Kilns, lab ovens and process units whose maker is gone cannot be re-tuned because nobody has the model. Praxion builds the model from experiments.

What not to claim

  • Do not attribute a share of the $1.4T downtime figure to control drift; no source isolates it.
  • Do not present the 5 to 15% energy figure as measured by Praxion. It is the literature's figure for human re-tuning. Praxion's own measured results are in docs/BENCHMARKS.md.
  • Do not use the "30% of HVAC energy is wasted" line. It comes from vendor blogs and sits above what the primary studies support.
