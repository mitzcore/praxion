## Classical self-tuning rival added to the benchmarks (2026-11-01)

**Why.** The competitive review (`docs/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md`) found the strongest skeptic line is "identify-and-redesign is a 1970s self-tuning regulator; relay auto-tuning is 1984." Our only adaptive-looking baseline was the hand-tuned slow PID, an existence proof. A fair rival had to be a fully automatic classical method, given the same plant access and re-identification opportunity as the agent, frozen before hidden-seed scoring.

**What.** `backend/evaluation/autotune.py`: Åström–Hägglund relay feedback → Tyreus–Luyben PI, two variants. `relay_pi` (nominal): from `reset(plant_seed)`, a full relay (u ∈ {0,1}) finds the operating point and a first ultimate point, then a symmetric relay (±0.2) around the measured operating point refines it; gains + output bias (the relay's mean u). `relay_pi_retuned`: the nominal PI is deployed under the frozen monitored rollout (`run_monitored_rollout`, family at 60 s, calibration 58 s, same `DegradationDetector`, 40 s post-detection); if the detector fires: hold the pre-change mean input until the level settles (≤ 60 s), then a bias-seeking relay (±0.1, one escalation to ±0.15) with a slope-based stuck test, then redesign; if it never fires, nominal gains are kept (Praxion's own rule for v1). `PIDController` gained `u_bias` (manual reset) inside the saturation logic; with the default 0.0 the frozen baselines are bit-identical (clamp branch kept exact). Benchmarks: `ALL_BASELINES` adds both rows to every ablation table, plus a new head-to-head section "Praxion vs the classical self-tuner" with post-change MAE, identification plant seconds for both sides, and cost. `autotune.py` is in the evaluator fingerprint.

**How the constants were chosen (exploration seeds 1–20 only, prototype in the session scratchpad).** Nominal: full relay oscillates on 20/20 seeds in 35–81 s with zero violations, Pu 6–14 s; two-stage + PI holds band 1.000 on all 20 (median MAE 0.0011 for TL-PI). Under `actuator_lag` the nominal relay-PI degrades on 20/20 seeds and the detector fires on 20/20 (107–158 s, i.e. 47–98 s after the change); under `pump_derate` it never fires (post MAE 0.001–0.003). Re-identification iterations: (1) full relay after the change overflows the tank on every seed (hundreds of hard-cutoff ticks), ±0.2 overflows on 4/8, ±0.1 is safe (max level ≤ 0.83) but needs 330–400 s, ±0.05 often never oscillates → ±0.1 with a 600 s cap. (2) Starting the relay straight after detection inherited a delay line full of saturated commands and the wrong bias → 11/20 failures; a settle hold fixed the first, but a 45 s "no switch" timeout fired falsely mid-cycle (half-period ≈ 40 s under the delay) → replaced by a slope test evaluated ≥ 45 s after a switch. (3) Bias from the oscillating detection window was unreliable; bias from the detector's calibration window (steady pre-change operation) gave 20/20 valid identifications with zero bias corrections, 289–410 s plant time, Pu 68–88 s, Ku 0.75–1.06. Rule criterion (pre-stated): highest worst-case in-band over both phases, tie-break nominal median MAE: ZN-PI 0.858, ZN-PID 0.912, TL-PI 1.000 (MAE 0.0011), TL-PID 1.000 (0.0013) → **TL-PI frozen**. Also tried and dropped: an automated step-test identification using exactly the agent's primitive (constant holds, 8 × 20 s / 4 × 40 s) with a one-step ARX fit → negative gains and the delay pinned at the grid edge on every seed (errors-in-variables on a noisy slow level). An output-error fitter would fix it; left as future work and recorded here so nobody re-learns it.

**Shipped-module validation (`uv run python scripts/validate_autotune.py`, log `artifacts/batch_logs/validate_autotune.log`, exploration seeds 1–20, plant-seed scenario).** Nominal: in-band 1.000 on 20/20, ≤ 123 s plant time, zero hard-cutoff ticks while tuning. `actuator_lag`: detector fired 20/20 (127–244 s, i.e. 67–184 s after the change), re-tuned 20/20, re-identification 177–394 s (60 s settle + 117–334 s relay), Pu 42–86 s, Ku 0.75–2.02, TL-PI kp 0.24–0.63 / ki 0.0012–0.0069; post-change in-band nominal 0.21–0.71 → re-tuned 1.00 on every seed; post MAE re-tuned 0.008–0.022 vs hand-tuned slow PID 0.006–0.024. Hard-cutoff ticks during re-identification 0–28 (mostly inherited from the saturated delay line at detection). Offset families: detector silent on 59/60 cases (one `drain_shift` seed fired and re-tuned; still in-band 1.00), so the classical system, like Praxion, does not re-tune when feedback already handles the change.

**Fairness, stated in the tables.** The relay switches in closed loop at 0.1 s, which the agent's experiment API cannot do; its plant-time cap is 600 s vs the agent's 160 s after-change budget; seconds actually used are reported side by side. Hard-cutoff ticks during identification are reported (most are inherited from the saturated delay line at detection time, which the agent's path also suffers in its 40 s post-detection window).

- *Mechanism, from the gains.* With Ti ≈ 180 s and kp ≈ 0.25 the re-tuned PI is close to an open-loop hold at the measured pre-change input, which is the correct steady input for a delay-only change. The exploration diagnostic below measures what that difference buys when a second disturbance follows the lag.
- *Reading.* On this plant and this metric the 1984 method wins, and the README says so. The result does not touch Praxion's claims (the agent authored, repaired and was gated; the repairs are real and inspectable) and sharpens the weakest link: repair *consistency*. Praxion's post-change MAE spans 0.010–0.193 across runs while the classical method spans 0.009–0.016; the median gap is what a protocol v1.4 iteration should target (the two runs with no completed re-identification, `spark5` and the no-revision `spark`/`spark2`, are the worst). It also shows what Praxion does that the relay cannot: change the controller structure, say why in words, produce a diff, and be gated by hidden scenarios.

**Second-disturbance diagnostic (exploration, not a gate; `uv run python scripts/diagnose_second_disturbance.py --sandbox docker`, 2026-11-02 00:39, output `artifacts/benchmarks/second_disturbance_diagnostic.{json,md}`).** Setup: the 9 runs with a promoted repair under `actuator_lag`; exploration scenario seeds 101–105 (hidden seeds untouched); `actuator_lag` at 60 s, then a second disturbance (pump_derate, extra_leak or drain_shift, seeded default magnitude) at 180 s; scored 210–420 s on true level; relay gains exactly as scored in `latest.json`; Praxion v2 sha-verified and run in Docker. 45 episodes per condition.

- *Lag only, replicated off the hidden seeds.* Relay re-tuned PI: median MAE 0.0099, in-band 1.000, 0 hard-cutoff ticks, 0 overrides in 45/45. Praxion v2: median MAE 0.0782, in-band 0.374, 19 hard-cutoff ticks and 84 overrides. Three of the nine repairs fail on these scenarios: `spark7` (seed 14; hidden-seed post MAE 0.0215, in-band 1.000) is overridden in 5/5 episodes and trips the cutoff in one; `v13-s3` (seed 3) trips the cutoff in 5/5; `spark5` (seed 11) empties the tank without an override (MAE 0.21). The other six repairs hold (MAE 0.003–0.08). The exploration scenarios are harsher than the five hidden ones in two ways we can name from the trusted audit: the seeded actuator time-constant factor is 1.12–1.40 vs 1.08–1.25, and two of five start at level 0.15–0.16 vs 0.26–0.41. **So a G3 pass on five hidden scenarios overstated the robustness of three of nine repairs.** That is a finding about the evaluator's statistical power, not about the frozen rule, and it is recorded here rather than fixed by changing the suite after the fact.
- *Second disturbance.* The relay re-tune behaves as the gains predict: it never regains the band (in-band 0.000 on all three families; recovered 0/45, 0/45 and 2/45; MAE 0.10–0.16; peak 0.14–0.21) and never touches the cutoff. Praxion v2 recovers the band within ~80–100 s in 47–58% of episodes (median MAE 0.08–0.10, in-band 0.23–0.37) and has the lower MAE in 78 of 135 episodes, but with 3–8 hard-cutoff ticks and 21–281 overrides per 45 episodes, almost all from the same three fragile repairs. The hand-tuned slow PID sits between (MAE 0.07–0.10, recovered 9–58%, 0–18 hard ticks).
- *Reading.* The classical re-tune's post-change MAE win is bought by giving up disturbance rejection: a slow loop around the right bias is the correct controller for the single-change suite and the wrong one for anything that happens next. Praxion's repairs keep loop authority and buy recovery, but three of nine are fragile enough to trip the trusted supervisor on scenarios the hidden set did not cover. Neither is a controller one would ship. For the protocol, two targets in order: (1) repair robustness, by having the agent's self-test and the evaluator score more than five scenarios (the frozen config stays frozen for the submission; the number is a parameter for the next version); (2) repair consistency across runs. For the pitch, the honest line is the one in the README: Praxion is the system that ran this rival inside its own evaluator and reports that it lost on the headline metric and why.

## Observations that changed the design

- **PI controllers reject offset disturbances by construction** (agent A, Oct 31). Pump derate, extra leak, drain shift are all absorbed by the fixed PID with post-change MAE 0.0026–0.0055 across the five benchmark machines (`docs/BENCHMARKS.md`). → Added the dynamics-changing family `actuator_lag` (tau ×1.0–1.5 plus 18–22 s transport delay). Slowing the actuator alone does not break PI; only pure delay does. Offset families stay in the benchmark to show the monitor correctly *not* firing.
- **A 20 s command delay defeats a reactive y-based cutoff** (overflow to 1.0 on 34/500 plant×scenario combos). → Independent high-level interlock on true level (ideal float switch) + delay-line flush on trip. Documented in `docs/SAFETY_LIMITATIONS.md`.
- **Degradation under delay grows slowly.** At 180 s the live v1 looks fine (post-disturbance MAE 0.017); at 300 s it limit-cycles (0.086, 53% in-band, 5 interlock trips). → `DISTURBANCE_EPISODE_S` 180 → 300 (frozen before any official scoring). Monitored live rollout 300 s.
- **Detector calibration mid-settling inflates the baseline** (0.025 at 45 s). → calibrate on the window ending at 58 s.
- **Detector floor:** sweep over 25 lag / 75 offset / 75 nominal cases (agent C): floor 0.05 catches 24/25 with 0 false alarms; the safe band is only 0.0435–0.0494. Kept 0.05; margins documented. Detection latency 86–114 s is inherent to the delay.
- **Plant vs scenario split.** The evaluator tests "this machine on unseen scenarios" (same hidden parameters; different initial level, noise stream, disturbance magnitudes), not different machines. Cross-machine evidence comes from repeating the protocol over plant seeds in Phase 4.
- **Spark readiness.** With zero evidence both models correctly say not ready; with 12 experiments Spark still never declared readiness. Experiment efficiency is therefore budget-bound for Spark; to be compared with Wonder.

- **Repairs failed because the agent could not measure the changed machine.** Muse only saw post-change traces at 5 s resolution and never ran an experiment on the changed plant; it under-estimated the delay 2–4× and designed for it. → **Protocol v1.1:** after degradation, up to 4 bounded re-identification experiments on the changed machine (phase `after_change`) before revising; post-change evidence traces at 150 points; revision prompt asks for a quantified change. Prompt hash b24d8a294e0ba81e.
- **Adaptation-latency metric** returned 0 s for controllers that were briefly in band then limit-cycled. → Redefined as settling time (start of the final in-band run lasting to episode end), before any official scoring.
